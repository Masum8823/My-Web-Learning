Bangladesh

Bangladesh, a densely populated nation in South Asia, spans 148,460 square kilometers and is home to over 174 million people, making it one of the world's most crowded countries. Bordered by India to the north, west, and east, Myanmar to the southeast, and the Bay of Bengal to the south, it features the fertile Ganges-Brahmaputra Delta, earning the nickname "land of rivers" with over 700 waterways. Dhaka, its bustling capital, is among the megacities globally, while Chittagong serves as the vital port.

The country boasts natural wonders like the Sundarbans, the planet's largest mangrove forest and UNESCO site, teeming with Bengal tigers, and Cox's Bazar, home to the longest unbroken sea beach at 120 km. Gaining independence in 1971 after a fierce Liberation War from Pakistan, Bangladesh has transformed from a war-torn state into a lower-middle-income economy, driven by its ready-made garments sector—the world's second-largest apparel exporter.

Agriculture thrives on rice, jute, and tea, supporting rural life, while remittances from overseas workers bolster growth. Poverty plummeted from 80% in 1971 to under 20% today, showcasing resilience against floods and cyclones. Bengali is the official language, Islam the dominant faith (90%), and it contributes significantly to UN peacekeeping. With a youthful population and rising literacy, Bangladesh eyes developed status by 2041.
